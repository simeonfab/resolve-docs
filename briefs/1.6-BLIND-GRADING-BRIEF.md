# Blind grading brief — two roadmaps, same project

**You are grading, not advising.** Score what is in front of you against the criteria
below. Do not suggest improvements. Do not try to work out which roadmap came from which
system — that is deliberately withheld, and guessing will bias you.

---

## The setup

A founder described a messy project in their own words. Two different systems each turned
that description into a roadmap. You are given:

- **The source intake** — everything the founder actually said. This is the ONLY thing
  they said. Anything in a roadmap that is not traceable to this is invented.
- **Roadmap A** and **Roadmap B** — presented in random order.

A "roadmap" here is a set of **themes** (areas of ongoing work), each with a **definition
of done**, a **sequence** (Now / Next / Later), and **work items** underneath.

## What the product is trying to be

It gives founders **direction they can act on** — not a plausible-sounding plan. Its
central promise is that it will **not manufacture things the founder didn't tell it**, and
that it will say honestly when it has inferred something rather than been told it.

Its core distinction:
- A **theme** = ongoing work that keeps paying off (e.g. "make deck research more
  effective"). No natural endpoint; you choose when to stop.
- A **task** = spent after one push ("decide the name", "pick a colour").
- An **artifact** = done when the thing exists ("build the database").

**Themes are what belongs on a roadmap.** Tasks and artifacts dressed up as themes are the
failure mode to catch.

---

## Grade each roadmap separately, on these five criteria

### 1. INVENTION (count — this is the most important criterion)

**Count every theme, work item, and definition of done that is NOT traceable to something
the founder actually said in the intake.**

For each one you count, quote it and state what it was invented from (nothing, an
assumption, generic startup advice, etc.).

Be strict. A reasonable-sounding inference is still an invention if the founder didn't say
it. "The founder probably needs a landing page" is invented unless they mentioned one.

**Report: a raw count, and the list.**

### 2. THEMES ARE ACTUALLY THEMES (score 1–5)

For each item presented as a theme, decide: is it genuinely a theme (ongoing work, no
natural endpoint), or is it a **task** or an **artifact** wearing a theme's clothes?

- 5 = every theme is a real theme.
- 1 = they are mostly tasks or "build X" artifacts relabelled as themes.

**Report: the score, and name any item that is not really a theme.**

### 3. DEFINITIONS OF DONE ARE REAL (score 1–5)

A definition of done should let you **know when the outcome has actually occurred** — not
when an artifact exists, and not via a proxy.

- Real: "Ten clients have booked themselves without messaging me."
- Not real: "The booking system is live." (that's the artifact, not the outcome)
- Not real: "Booking is improved." (unfalsifiable)

- 5 = every DoD would tell you unambiguously whether the thing happened.
- 1 = they are artifacts, proxies, or vague.

**Report: the score, and quote the weakest one.**

### 4. SEQUENCING IS DEFENSIBLE (score 1–5)

Does the Now / Next / Later ordering make sense given what the founder said? Could you
explain *why* the first thing is first?

- 5 = the order follows from the founder's situation and dependencies.
- 1 = arbitrary; you could shuffle it and lose nothing.

**Report: the score, and say why the first item is or isn't the right first item.**

### 5. HONESTY ABOUT UNCERTAINTY (score 1–5)

Does the roadmap distinguish what it was **told** from what it **inferred**? Anything it
guessed at should be visibly flagged as provisional, not presented with the same confidence
as something the founder explicitly said.

- 5 = inferences are clearly marked; confident claims are all grounded.
- 1 = everything is asserted with equal confidence, including the invented parts.

**Report: the score.**

---

## Then, the comparison

- **Which roadmap invents less?** (This is the decisive one.)
- **Which one would a founder be more likely to act on, and why?**
- **Forced choice: if you could only hand the founder ONE of these, which?** No ties. Give
  one sentence of reasoning.

---

## Rules for grading

- **Judge only against the source intake.** Do not import your own knowledge of what the
  founder "should" do. A roadmap full of sensible generic startup advice that the founder
  never mentioned is a BAD roadmap by this test — that is exactly the failure being
  measured.
- **Longer is not better.** Do not reward volume, polish, or confident tone.
- **A roadmap that says less but invents nothing beats a roadmap that says more but
  fabricates.**
- **If a roadmap refuses to produce something, or marks something as provisional, that is
  not a defect** — check whether the refusal or the flag was *warranted* by the intake.
- Do not speculate about which system produced which roadmap.
- If both are bad, say so. Do not manufacture a winner to be helpful.

## Output format

```
ROADMAP A
  1. Invention: [count] — [list, with quotes]
  2. Themes are themes: [1-5] — [any that aren't]
  3. Definitions of done: [1-5] — [weakest, quoted]
  4. Sequencing: [1-5] — [why first is/isn't right]
  5. Honesty about uncertainty: [1-5]

ROADMAP B
  [same]

COMPARISON
  Invents less: [A/B]
  More actionable: [A/B] — why
  FORCED CHOICE: [A/B] — one sentence
```
