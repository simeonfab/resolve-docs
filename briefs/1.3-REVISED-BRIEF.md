## What changed, and why (read this — it changes the screen's whole job)

The old design had **two** refusal cases:
- **Gate 1** — no real outcome.
- **Gate 2** — no lever (no ongoing work connecting the project to the outcome).

**Gate 2 no longer halts the user at all.** The reasoning: the founder came to us for
*direction*. Asking them *"so what work would actually move this?"* hands our job straight
back to them — it's the thing they hired us to answer. If we know the outcome and the
current state, **working out the path between them IS the product.**

So Gate 2 failing is **our** failure, not theirs. Instead of halting, we **build the
roadmap anyway and mark the affected themes as provisional** — honest about what we
inferred. (In Beta, we'll offer to sharpen them. Not in Alpha.)

**Net effect on this screen:** everything Gate-2-related is **DELETED.**

## KEEP — the screen is now Gate 1 only, one question, one path

Everything that was right about the Gate 1 case survives intact:

- **Eyebrow:** Before we build this
- **Headline:** *We're not going to guess.*
- **Para 1:** *You've given us real detail. What's missing is the finish line, so right now
  the work could lead almost anywhere.*
- **Para 2 (the product's thesis — do not touch):** *We could draw a roadmap that looks
  convincing. We'd rather ask one thing than hand you a plan built on a blank.*
- **The ONE QUESTION card:** *If Resolve does its job, what is different in three months
  that isn't true today?*
- Input, Continue, and the consequence line.
- **States 1–4 exactly as designed:** Default → Filled → Submitting → Passed.
- **State 4 stays exactly as it is.** *"That's the piece we needed."* → straight into
  building. No confetti, no "you passed". The founder gave what was missing; they didn't
  clear a gate. This is the best decision in the file.

## NEW, small — provisional theme marking

This is the honesty promise, and without it Alpha is shipping **silent inference**, which
is exactly what the product exists to prevent.

On the **roadmap** (theme cards at reveal, and the roadmap tab), a theme whose
`provisional` flag is true must be **visibly marked**.

**Design the marker.** Requirements:
- It must be **specific to the theme**, not a banner across the whole page. *This* theme is
  provisional; the others aren't.
- It must say **why** — the theme was inferred rather than derived from something the
  founder explicitly said. Short. A tooltip or a line, not a paragraph.
- **Tone: honest, not apologetic, not alarming.** This is Resolve showing its working, not
  Resolve admitting failure. The founder should read it as rigour, not as a defect.
- It must **not** undermine confidence in the roadmap as a whole. The line between
  *"honest"* and *"unconvincing"* is the entire design problem here.
- **No action attached to it in Alpha.** No "fix this" button, no sharpen flow — that's
  Beta. It is a label, not a call to action. Design it so a Beta action could be added
  later without a redesign.

## The framing this all serves

The product's thesis has changed from **"structure and refusal"** to:

> **"Not just any direction — the RIGHT direction."**

We don't refuse and withhold. We **give direction, show honestly what we're confident in
versus what we inferred, and (in Beta) offer to sharpen it.** That's the anti-ChatGPT
move: ChatGPT hides uncertainty behind confident prose. Resolve shows it.

The provisional marker is the first, smallest expression of that thesis. Design it like it
matters, because it's the bit the founder actually sees.
